# Dataset Generation Methodology

## Reproducibility

Every generator script calls `random.seed(20260915)` before any random draw and reads its inputs in a fixed order, so a clean rerun of the full pipeline (phases 1→4, in order, against a clean environment) reproduces this exact dataset byte-for-byte. Adding or removing any random draw anywhere upstream shifts everything downstream — this is why the scripts must be run in the documented order and not edited casually.

## Phase 1 — Master / reference tables (`generate_master_tables.py`)

- **industries, territories, departments, lead_sources, products**: small fixed catalogs (20, 18, 7, 12, 20 rows respectively), hand-curated to be internally consistent and India-appropriate (e.g. GST-relevant industry categories, realistic SaaS/enterprise-software product catalog with INR list prices).
- **sales_reps** (80 rows): generated with an explicit management hierarchy — 1 Director → 6 Managers → a pool of Senior/Account Executives, SDRs, and Customer Success Managers reporting to those managers. Hire dates, active/terminated status (~8% terminated), and territory/department assignment are drawn from weighted distributions designed to look like a real 3-year-old sales org.
- **campaigns** (180 rows): campaign type, budget, and duration drawn per-type (e.g. trade shows run longer and cost more than a single email blast), spread across fiscal quarters.
- **companies** (1,200 rows, the pilot batch): two generation paths —
  - **Real-company anchor rows** (`name_is_real_entity = True`, ~8%): drawn from a curated list of ~96 real, publicly known Indian companies with their real industry and city as the only "real" facts carried forward. Every other field (GSTIN, revenue band, pincode, website domain, size, contacts, and all downstream CRM activity) is synthetically generated exactly like any other row.
  - **Synthetic companies** (~92%): names are procedurally generated via a weighted pattern choice — 45% "surname + sector word" (e.g. "Bhopal Transport Co"), 35% "city + sector word", 20% "generic adjective + sector word" — drawn from large per-industry word pools so names read as plausible Indian SMB/enterprise names without repeating. Company size, revenue band, and type (Private Limited/LLP/Partnership/Proprietorship/PSU/Public Limited) are drawn from realistic joint distributions (e.g. Proprietorship and Partnership are capped to Micro/Small/Mid — a sole proprietorship is never generated as "Enterprise" size).

## Phase 2 — Contacts, leads, lead history, campaign responses (`generate_contacts_leads.py`)

- **contacts**: 0–4 contacts per company depending on company size (larger companies get more), with department/seniority drawn from weighted distributions, and job titles looked up from a seniority×department matrix. Founder/Owner-type contacts are more likely at Micro/Small companies.
- **leads**: whether a company produces any leads, and how many, is driven by size- and industry-specific probability/count weights, with seasonality (monthly weighting) applied to lead-creation dates across a ~3-year window.
- **The funnel walk** (the core realism mechanism): each lead is simulated as a sequential state machine — New → Contacted → Qualifying → Qualified (MQL) → Sales Qualified (SQL) — where, at each stage, a per-stage probability decides whether the lead advances, stalls (goes to Nurture/Dead), or gets disqualified (with a stage-appropriate disqualification reason distribution — e.g. early-stage disqualifications skew toward "no budget"/"not a fit," later-stage toward "chose competitor"/"budget cut"). Dwell time between stages is drawn from a distribution scaled by a company-size and industry cycle-length multiplier (larger companies and certain industries take longer to move through the funnel), and every stage transition is recorded as a `lead_status_history` row — the history table is a byproduct of the simulation, not generated independently, which is what keeps it internally consistent.
- **campaign_responses**: generated for leads/contacts that came from a campaign-attributed source, with a response-type distribution appropriate to the campaign type.

## Phase 3 — Opportunities, customers, transactions, support cases (`generate_opps_customers.py`)

- **Opportunity creation**: only leads that reached **Sales Qualified (SQL)** are eligible. A `lead_quality_index()` function combines source quality, company-size quality, industry fit, decision-maker involvement, contact seniority, budget presence, and rep assignment into a single score, which sets the probability that a given SQL lead actually produces an opportunity (30–90%, size-adjusted). The opportunity's `created_at` is anchored to the lead's *actual* Sales-Qualified transition timestamp (not the earlier MQL timestamp — an anchor bug that produced impossible date sequences was found and fixed during audit; see `data_quality_report.md`).
- **The stage walk**: a 7-stage pipeline (Qualification → Discovery → Solution Fit → Proposal → Negotiation → Closed Won/Closed Lost) simulated the same way as the lead funnel — sequential, stochastic, stage-appropriate advance/stall/lose probabilities, dwell time scaled by company-size and industry cycle-length multipliers, every transition recorded to `opportunity_stage_history`. Win/loss probabilities were calibrated (recalibrated once, from an initial 12.5% win rate up to the ~45% seen in this batch) to land in a realistic range for a validated, sales-qualified pipeline. `probability_pct` is tied deterministically to `pipeline_stage` (100/0 at the closed stages, a fixed band per open stage).
- **opportunity_products (line items)**: 1–4 products per opportunity, weighted by company size (larger companies buy more/bigger SKUs), with a discount drawn from a realistic tier distribution (0/5/10/15/20%, weighted toward no/low discount). `deal_value_inr` is always the exact sum of its line items — never independently drawn.
- **Customer creation**: **only** from a Closed-Won opportunity, at the same company. `customer_since` is the close date plus a short invoicing/onboarding lag, clamped to never exceed the generation cutoff date. Customer tier is a direct function of deal value; contract type (Monthly/Annual/Multi-Year) is drawn from a distribution and sets the renewal cadence.
- **customer_transactions**: an initial "New Sale" transaction equal to the opportunity's deal value, followed by a renewal-cycle simulation — at each cadence checkpoint, a renewal probability (driven by an "engagement tier" derived from support-case history and payment behavior) decides whether the customer renews, and independently whether they upsell, downgrade, or occasionally get refunded. This is what produces natural churn: customers simply stop generating renewal transactions rather than being flagged by a status field.
- **customer_support_cases**: case volume and priority mix vary by customer tier and tenure; resolution time is drawn per priority (Critical cases resolve faster on average), and satisfaction score is populated only for a resolved-case subset.

## Phase 4 — ML-ready datasets (`generate_ml_datasets.py`)

Each of the 5 datasets is built by reading the validated Phase 1–3 tables and applying an explicit **observation cutoff** per row, then computing every feature using only source-table data timestamped at or before that cutoff (see `ml_leakage_prevention.md` for the full audit of this). Labels are derived from the eventual, full-history outcome in the source tables (e.g. "did this lead ever reach Converted," "was there a renewal transaction in the checkpoint's grace window") — never invented independently of the source tables. `dataset_split` is assigned chronologically (train = earliest observations, test = latest) rather than randomly, so a model trained on this data is evaluated the way it would actually be deployed: on data that is later in time than what it was trained on.

## Realism techniques used throughout

- **Sequential state-machine simulation** (funnel walk, stage walk, renewal-cycle walk) instead of independently sampling each field — this is what makes cross-field logic (dates, status transitions, probabilities) consistent by construction rather than needing to be patched after the fact.
- **MNAR-style missingness**: fields like `estimated_budget_inr`, `linkedin_handle`, and `phone` are blank not at random but correlated with realistic causes (e.g. budget is less often captured for early-stage/low-quality leads).
- **Weighted categorical distributions** everywhere a real business would show skew (company size, lead source, disqualification reason, payment status) rather than uniform random choice.
- **Cycle-length multipliers** by company size and industry, applied consistently to lead dwell time, opportunity dwell time, and renewal cadence, so an Enterprise logistics deal takes visibly longer end-to-end than a Micro retail deal — a property that shows up correctly in the ML datasets' `avg_deal_cycle_days_trailing_6mo` and similar features without being hard-coded there.
